What Uptick shows

Uptick tracks your watchlist against a live (or simulated) price feed. Each ticker's close price is plotted against a configurable simple moving average (SMA), with the gap between the two shown as a histogram underneath — a quick read on whether a ticker is running hot above its own trend or cooling off below it.

The four chips in the top-right are quick market context — ^IXIC (Nasdaq Composite), NQ=F (Nasdaq futures), CL=F (crude oil futures), and ^VIX — shown at a glance, same as any watchlist ticker under the hood.

Today / Positions cards

TODAY's own % reads against a daily $ goal when one's set in Settings — e.g. a $150 gain against a $300 goal reads as 50%, not a tiny fraction of total account equity. Falls back to the plain equity-based % whenever no goal is configured, same as it's always worked.

Symbol groups

The row of chips above the ticker bar are named snapshots of which symbols are currently selected — not separate watchlists. Your actual watchlist (the full ticker roster) is never touched by any of this.

  • Click a group to switch to it — replaces your current selection outright
  • Save group saves whatever's currently checked under a name you type
  • Saving under an existing name updates that group instead of creating a duplicate — saving under a new name instead is a quick way to clone one group into another
  • The small on a chip deletes that saved group only — your watchlist symbols aren't affected

Close

The last traded price at each interval, plotted as the black line.

SMA

SMA(n) = average of the last n closes

The blue line — the reference trend the delta bars measure against. n is set in Settings.

Delta bars

Delta = Close − SMA

Green when price is running above its SMA, red when below. Scaled small on purpose so it reads as a strip, not a second chart.

Hourly marks

Solid, light grey, unlabeled vertical lines at 10am–3pm on the Today chart — a quiet reference grid for the regular trading session, distinct from the dashed pink Open/Close lines at 9:30/4:00.

Combined

One combined line: the average % change (from the start of the window) across every ticker currently selected — a quick read on how the whole selected basket is moving together, not any single name.

Compare

Every selected ticker side by side, each with its own Close/SMA/delta chart, same as the Combined chart but per-ticker.

Top-right of each tile, when not held: today's own % change, plus — in a second, aqua-colored number — how today compares to yesterday's close, whenever that's been captured. Captured once a day, at 15:55, for whatever's actually selected/showing on this view at that exact moment — not the full watchlist, and not tied to which symbols are bolt-enabled. No master-switch dependency either; any device open at that moment captures its own values independently, so different devices may end up with slightly different numbers if they weren't all showing the same selection at the time. A symbol never captured this way just shows the first number alone.

Table

For the current timeframe window: the price at the start of the chart, the price at the end, and the $ / % delta between them — not a day-over-day number.

Research

A one-off symbol lookup, separate from your watchlist — search any symbol, see its chart at any timeframe, without adding it or affecting your selected tickers. Purely for research: there's no path from here to your real watchlist. Ticker selection and symbol groups both grey out and have no effect while this view is active. Not saved between sessions — the searched symbol resets on reload.

Picking a past date

The calendar icon next to Today opens a date picker. Pick a date, and every timeframe — Today, 10D, 30D, and so on — treats that date as "today": the same window it would normally show, just anchored to a different day.

Click the Today label (not the calendar icon) to return to live, or just wait — it resets on its own after 60 minutes with no clicks anywhere in the app, or automatically once the next trading day's 4am boundary passes, whichever comes first. The Today label itself turns amber with an explanatory tooltip while a historical date is active, as a reminder that it's not just Buy/Sell that's paused underneath — automated sell/max-loss monitoring on anything you're holding is paused too, until you're back on live.

While browsing a past date:

  • Buy/Sell and Last 6H — disabled (greyed out, not just click-rejected)
  • Chart auto-refresh — paused, since history doesn't change
  • Held-position dot / $/% gain — hidden, since those reflect your current position, not the picked date
  • Account/positions — stay fully live regardless, since your real balance is a current-day fact either way

5-minute intraday data ("Today") isn't retained indefinitely by most data providers — a date far enough in the past may come back empty even though 10D/30D/etc. for that same date work fine. That's a data availability limit, not a bug.

Account

A bar per trading day for the last 14 days — today's realized P&L, straight from Alpaca's own portfolio history, not derived from anything else in the app. A second, lighter line tracks the cumulative total across the same window.

  • Bars are colored the same green/red as everywhere else in the app — no separate convention to learn
  • A dashed reference line appears if a daily $ target is set in Settings — purely visual, never read by any trading logic
  • The window can be clipped to a chosen start date in Settings, so history from before you started using the bot doesn't dilute the picture — it can only ever shrink the 14-day window, never extend past it
  • Fetches once when you switch to this view — the Refresh button re-pulls on demand; nothing here auto-updates on its own timer

Data source

Switch between simulated sample data (for building/demoing) and your real backend. Sample mode regenerates a random-walk series locally; API mode calls the endpoints below. Symbol data and account data can point at two entirely different services.

Purpose Endpoint
Series GET {activeSymbolSourceUrl}/{symbol}/{timeframe}/{startISO}/{endISO}
Account GET {accountBaseUrl}/v2/account
Positions GET {accountBaseUrl}/v2/positions
Buy POST {accountBaseUrl}/v2/orders
Sell DELETE {accountBaseUrl}/v2/positions/{symbol}?percentage=100
Liquidate DELETE {accountBaseUrl}/v2/positions

Symbol data supports a Primary and a Backup source with the same contract — switch between them from a Settings toggle if one is having issues, without losing either URL. An optional Token, if set, is appended as ?token= on Primary requests only — a light gatekeeping measure your own backend can check against an allowlist, not encryption or real security.

{timeframe} is 5Min for Today or 1D for 10D/30D. {startISO}/{endISO} are full ISO timestamps; end always defaults to the end of the current calendar day.

Expected series response: {"bars":{"SYMBOL":[{"o":n,"h":n,"l":n,"c":n,"t":"ISO string","e":epochMs,"v":n}, ...]}} — the symbol key sits under a top-level bars object. Only t and c are used today; SMA is computed client-side from c.

This is Alpaca's own API shape throughout (/v2/account, /v2/positions, /v2/orders). Every request sends your API key/secret as APCA-API-KEY-ID and APCA-API-SECRET-KEY headers — Alpaca's actual header names, not a generic placeholder. Stored only in this browser's local storage, never written into the app's source.

Today & Positions

Today comes from the account resource's equity and last_equity fields (today's change = equity − last_equity) — the account-level number, not derived from individual positions.

Positions comes from the positions resource: an array, one entry per open position, each with cost_basis and unrealized_pl (numeric strings). Aggregated dollar-weighted across every returned position, not averaged per-position.

Account equity and buying power also show as small blue text next to the clock, above the stat cards — reads "-" for either in Sample mode, since there's no real account behind it to report on.

Buy/Sell buttons

Sit at the bottom of every Compare tile, icon-only — hover for a tooltip.

  • Grey — buy $100
  • Teal — buy $1,000
  • Green — buy $10,000
  • Red — sell (full position)

The amount is editable via a prompt before confirming; Sell is gated behind a confirm dialog. Neither fires without that explicit step, and neither actually places an order until Live API mode is fully configured — otherwise clicking opens Settings instead.

Liquidate account

The small icon in the top-right corner of the Positions card — not per-symbol, exists once. It closes every open position immediately, gated behind a confirm dialog.

Visual cues

Five-minute highlight — the Today card gets a steady blue border for the full minute at each :00/:05/:10/... mark, while the market's live. Deliberately not a flash or pulse — the point is a reliable, easy-to-notice checkpoint cue, not another source of reactive urgency.

Loss alert — a red hue appears on the POSITIONS card, and independently on each row and the TOTAL in the Positions table, whenever that figure's % change drops below your configured threshold (default -0.5%, adjustable in Settings). Runs on the account refresh cadence, so it stays current around the clock, not just during trading hours.

Automated trading

Hidden here in Sample mode or an unconfigured setup — this only shows once Live trading is actually ready, since nothing below applies otherwise.

Master switch — local to this browser/device only, not a network setting. Off by default.

  • Nothing evaluates or executes while it's off, regardless of any symbol's bolt state
  • The header's own bolt icon fills solid blue whenever this is on

The bolt icon on each Compare tile opts that symbol in or out.

  • Grey — off
  • Dim blue — armed: enabled, but outside your buy window right now
  • Full blue — active: enabled and within the window
  • Always clickable, even on a historical date — turning a symbol off is never blocked
  • Enabling it on a symbol that's already moving is picked up on the very next check, not just a fresh move from that point forward

Buying and selling both work the same way: state-based, not a one-time trigger. Every minute, each enabled symbol is asked one question — does its current delta bar say it should be owned right now?

  • Should be owned, not already held, buy window open, no-buy override off, enough buying power → buys
  • Held, but the bar says it shouldn't be → sells (buy window doesn't apply to selling — a held position needs an exit any time the market's open)

This replaced an earlier design that only acted on a fresh change in direction — that rule could miss a real move entirely if no clean flip existed at the exact moment it happened to check.

A held position also sells on either of two independent triggers, regardless of what the delta bar says:

  • Its own loss crossing your configured Max Loss threshold (can differ before/after 11:30 AM — see below)
  • A day cap being hit (see below)

Turning a symbol's bolt off also stops watching any position you're holding in it — not just new buying. You'll get a one-time notice when this applies to something you currently hold; the toggle itself always succeeds regardless.

Deadband — how far price has to move from its own trend line before it counts as a real signal rather than noise; anything inside it reads neutral. Morning/default and gain/loss can each use different values — leave the second field of either pair empty to use the same value throughout.

Position max loss (%) can differ before 11:30 AM versus the rest of the day — a fixed cutoff. A position held across that boundary starts using the other threshold the moment the clock crosses it, regardless of when it was opened.

Invest $ is capped at $5,000 during this testing phase — hardcoded, not something a larger Settings number can override. A buy is also skipped, silently and safely, if buying power doesn't actually cover the configured amount.

Max gain cap ($) — account-level, not per-symbol. Once today's total gain reaches this, sells every bolt-enabled held position and locks out new buying for the rest of the trading day.

Max loss/gain-protect cap ($) — same lockout mechanism as above, but a direct signed threshold rather than always a loss magnitude below zero.

  • A negative value protects against loss, same as before (e.g. -20 locks out once today drops to -$20)
  • A positive value protects an existing gain instead — locks out if today's total drops below that figure (e.g. +15 locks out if a $25 gain slips back to $12)
  • Useful specifically after re-enabling via the clear toggle below, mid-day, once Max Gain has already tripped — protects whatever's already banked rather than only ever limiting fresh downside from zero

Once either cap trips:

  • Sells every bolt-enabled held position individually
  • Locks out new buying for the rest of the trading day — even if the sell itself nudges today back across the line afterward
  • Resets at the next trading day's 4:00 AM boundary — or clear it yourself sooner (see below)
  • Never affects manual trading, either way

The cap fields themselves turn amber in Settings while a real cap trigger is currently locking out new buys. The routine end-of-day close uses this same lockout mechanism, but that's normal daily behavior and isn't flagged the same way.

Day cap lockout — Off/On toggle, right below No buy today. "On" is never a real choice — it's disabled, since only the bot itself ever sets it, reflecting whatever's actually happening. "Off" is a genuine action: clearing an active lockout yourself.

  • Clearing it doesn't undo the underlying cause on its own — if today's numbers still exceed whatever caps are currently configured, the very next check re-locks it automatically
  • Raising the caps first (or the day's numbers genuinely no longer exceeding them) is what actually makes the clear stick
  • Built specifically for a deliberate, watched day — a cap trips as expected, you're confident the rest of the day still looks good, and want the bot back engaged rather than stuck manual-only until tomorrow

No buy today — a separate switch from the master one, freely reversible any time.

  • Blocks new buys only, for the rest of today
  • Anything already held keeps being watched and sold exactly as normal
  • Meant for calling a day "done" before anything has traded — undo it just as easily if that read turns out wrong
